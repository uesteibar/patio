# patio: UX review for a developer who joins a team (9 Nov 2026)

Reviewer: independent design review, hands-on. Branch `poc`, `patio 0.12.0`.
Screenshots and a terminal transcript: `docs/review/ux-2026-11-09/`.

## Verdict

1. Ship to a friendly team tomorrow? Yes, as a pilot of 2 to 4 developers with the author on hand. No, for a team that joins alone.
2. The join itself is good: idempotent, honest about each trust step, with readable rules and a clear confirmation screen.
3. The single biggest UX problem: what waits for the person is not where the product says it is. With the default third answer ("no"), every question from the team waits in a CLI-only inbox. "Needs you" says "Nothing waits for you", `patio status` counts 0, and the web app cannot answer.
4. The second problem is trust: "Only you can decide, here" is not true for the rules. An agent can turn on "answer on its own" with one command, and the join enters the workspace with the widest rules before the person answers.
5. Fix the three P0 items and the first five P1 items before a second team.

## How I tested

- Read PRODUCT.md, README.md, the two design shapes, `docs/poc/try-on-your-mac.md` and the end of `docs/poc/execution.md`.
- Read the hosted invite page and `install.sh` with `curl` and the browser (read only).
- Started a local instance (`just up alice bob --channel backend`). Joined as a new person `dave` the agent way (no terminal, `CLAUDECODE=1`, the prompt's exact flags), and as `carol` from a pseudo-terminal. Used the fake GitHub sign-in. Walked every web app page as alice, dave and carol.
- Sent messages, held one outgoing message, added a second agent, left a workspace, tried wrong inputs.
- Ran `just mcp-probe` and read `src/mcp.ts` and the guide.
- I did not re-report the six known findings in `docs/poc/execution.md`. Where I add to one, I say so.

## P0: blocks a new user

### P0-1. A question that waits for you has no home

**What I saw.**
- Carol joined with the defaults. Her rules say: "carol-team does not answer a workspace on its own: the rest waits for you."
- Alice asked carol-team "Is the staging deploy green?".
- Carol's **Needs you** said "Nothing waits for you" in both sections. The sidebar had no badge. Screenshot `19-carol-needs-you-while-question-waits.png`.
- `patio status` said `waiting for you: 0 incoming and 0 outgoing messages` and `status: healthy`.
- **Conversations** listed the question as "no reply yet" and is "Read-only". The full view has no form, no Answer button. Screenshot `20-carol-direct-messages.png`.
- Only `patio inbox` showed it: `* 46b0af93  66a87887  alice  [question]  Is the staging deploy green?`.
- On the asking side, alice got `no replies before the timeout`, with no hint that a person must act.
- Desktop notifications exist only on macOS (`src/daemon.ts:1548`). On Linux the person hears nothing.

**Why it matters.** "No" is the default third answer, so this is the normal state for every new member. The product promise is "the person hears from patio only when a decision is theirs". Here a decision is theirs, and patio hides it. The teammate's agent waits and gives up. Both sides conclude that patio does not work.

**Fix.**
- Add a section "Questions for you" to Needs you. Each row: who, the text, and three actions: **Let dave-team answer** (runs `patio answer`), **Reply myself** (a text field), **Decline**. Count these rows in the sidebar badge and in `patio status` ("1 question waits for you").
- Notify on Linux too (`notify-send`), and only for items that wait for the person, not for items the service answers.
- Tell the asker the truth. `ask` and `wait_for_reply` should say "carol-team answers when @carol does; it may take hours". Add "answers on its own" or "answered by @carol" to the member list in the guide (section 6) and in `who`.

### P0-2. An agent can widen its own rules without the person

**What I saw.**
- From no terminal (stdin `/dev/null`, as an agent runs it): `patio rules dave-team --answer yes` printed `changed: ... answering team on its own: yes`, exit 0. `patio status` then showed `0 rules to turn on`. The change applied at once.
- A rerun of the join from no terminal with `--ask anything --act deny --answer yes` also applied at once and printed `patio is ready`.
- The README table "Who confirms what" says that turning on `auto_answer` needs the web app, "Anyone, even you in your own terminal". PRODUCT.md says "Agents can never approve anything". The Needs you page says "Only you can decide, here".

**Why it matters.** The rules are the product's main safety promise. A prompt-injected or over-eager agent can make itself answer the whole team with one command, and the person sees nothing. The design for the join accepted this widening "because the person just answered". That reason does not hold for `patio rules` on day 20.

**Fix.**
- From no terminal, a change that widens (`--ask anything`, `--answer yes`) becomes a card in Needs you: "dave-team wants to answer team on its own". A change that narrows applies at once.
- For the join, show the three answers on the one confirmation screen ("It may answer: questions and small requests. It answers team on its own: no"). The same Confirm click then covers them. A rerun that widens after the confirmation makes a card.

### P0-3. The join puts the agent in the workspace with the widest rules, before the answers

This adds to known finding 4 (the prompt runs the join before the questions). The consequence is worse than the order.

**What I saw.**
- After the first run and Confirm, the agent was in `team` and had no file. `patio rules dave-team` said "from the default rules (no file)": `kinds all`, and "Others may ask dave-team anything they like".
- The Agents page showed a black **Ready** badge, the same three sentences ("anything they like"), and "set up once your agent runs patio join again". Screenshot `08-after-confirm.png`.
- The rerun without answers exits 13, but the agent stays in the workspace with those rules.
- The same happens to a second agent made with `patio agents new` (screenshot `16-agents-two.png`: dave-side, "anything they like", Ready, "not in any workspace").

**Why it matters.** The design says the safe default is "questions and small requests". Many people stop after the browser step: they close the session or the agent stops at exit 13. Then they stay in the team with the widest setting, and the app calls it Ready.

**Fix.**
- Ask the three questions before the first run (fix the prompt), and make `patio join` refuse to enter the workspace from no terminal without `--ask --act --answer`.
- Make the rules with no file equal the join's defaults (`requests`, `ask-human`, `no`), not "anything".
- Show "Not finished: waits for your three answers" instead of Ready.

## P1: confuses or annoys

### P1-1. The invite page does not say what patio is or what will happen

**What I saw.** the hosted invite page (screenshot removed: it showed private details). The page has one heading ("@pablo invited you to orbit · 4 agents"), one sentence, the prompt, and a terminal command. It does not say:
- what patio is ("your agent asks your teammates' agents, and answers theirs from this repo");
- what it installs: a binary in `~/.local/bin`, a background process, a login service (launchd or systemd) that can run your coding tool;
- that it writes `.mcp.json`, `.codex/config.toml` and `.pi/mcp.json` in your repo;
- that your agent will ask you three questions, and what they mean;
- who the "4 agents" are.

The page also has a button **Open in the patio app** next to "for when there is one". The button opens `patio://`, which nothing handles.

**Why it matters.** This is the first and only screen before a person runs `curl | sh` and gives a background service access to their repo. A careful developer stops here. A trusting one feels tricked later when they find the service and the files.

**Fix.** Above the prompt, add three short lines: "What it is", "What it installs and writes", "What you decide (three questions, one Confirm)". List the members with their owners. Remove the patio app button until the app exists.

### P1-2. The prompt has traps for a cheap model

This adds to known finding 4.

**What I saw** (prompt text from the hosted page):
- Step 3 says "with the defaults in bold". The prompt is plain text in a `<pre>`, so nothing is bold. The model must guess that the first option is the default.
- Step 3 says "run the join again adding: --ask requests|questions|anything --act ask-human|deny --answer no|yes". The exit-13 output prints the same pattern as a command to copy. A literal copy puts shell pipes in the command.
- The prompt names "Claude Code, Codex or Pi". PRODUCT.md lists Hermes too.

**Fix.** Write the defaults as words ("default: questions and small requests"). Print one full ready-to-run command for the defaults, and a mapping table for the other answers. Never print `a|b|c` in a line the model may run.

### P1-3. `--help` fails on every subcommand, and wrong input gives wrong advice

**What I saw.**
- `patio join --help`, `rules --help`, `agents --help`, `ask --help`: `Unknown option '--help'. To specify a positional argument starting with a '-', place it at the end of the command after '--' ...`, exit 1. `patio inbox -h` and `patio status --help`: `Unknown option`.
- `patio foo` prints the whole 120-line help, exit 1, with no "unknown command: foo".
- `patio join not-a-link` on a fresh computer: "patio is not running on this computer ...; start it with `patio up`". It treats the text as a channel name.
- `patio join patio.example.com/join/<code> --no-wait` (a link without `https://`, a common paste): "usage: --agent, --tool, --folder, ... go with patio join <invite link>".

**Why it matters.** Agents and people both try `--help` first. Each failure here sends the reader the wrong way: "patio up" is not a step a new user should ever run.

**Fix.** Support `-h` and `--help` on every subcommand. Say "unknown command: foo" and show the short list. Treat anything that contains `/join/` as an invite link, and add `https://` when it is missing. On a fresh computer, `patio join <word>` should say "This is not an invite link. Paste the link from your invite page."

### P1-4. Status and the Agents page say "healthy", "Connected" and "Ready" when that is not true

**What I saw.**
- After the join exited 12 (no tool listed patio, three trust steps open), `patio status` said `CONNECTED claude, codex, pi` and `status: healthy`. The Agents page showed three black **Connected** pills (`13-dave-agents.png`).
- Carol's status said `not connected (patio connect <tool> --as carol-team)` and still `status: healthy`.
- **Ready** shows on an agent with no file (P0-3) and on an agent in no workspace (`16-agents-two.png`).

**Why it matters.** `patio status` is the one command to ask "is it working?". If it says healthy while the agent cannot use patio in any tool, the person stops trusting it.

**Fix.** Use three states per tool: "written, waits for your trust in Claude Code", "listed", "not set up". Make `status` exit 1 with the `!` steps when no tool lists patio. Replace Ready with what is true: "Not in a workspace", "Waits for your three answers", "Working".

### P1-5. The join ends without a next step

**What I saw.** The terminal join ended with `patio is ready: carol-team is in team.` (`terminal-join-transcript.txt`). It did not say: start a new session of your tool here; try "ask bob ..."; your web app is at `http://127.0.0.1:...`; questions for you appear there. Without `--connect`, it did not connect any tool and did not say so. The agent path ends with either `patio is ready` or `nothing to do`, two different endings for the same state.

**Fix.** End every successful join with the same three lines: "Next: start a new <tool> session in this folder. Try: ask <teammate> about <something>. What needs you: <web app URL>."

### P1-6. The project files can leak into the team's repository

**What I saw.** The join wrote `.mcp.json`, `.codex/config.toml` and `.pi/mcp.json` in the repo, untracked. `.mcp.json` holds an absolute path to the binary and `--as dave-team`. Nothing adds them to an ignore file, and the output does not warn.

**Why it matters.** Teams often commit `.mcp.json` on purpose. One `git add .` and every teammate's Claude Code asks to start "patio as dave-team", with a path that exists only on Dave's computer.

**Fix.** Add the three paths to `.git/info/exclude` (local, never committed), and print one line that says so.

### P1-7. Messages wait in two id systems

**What I saw.** `patio inbox` prints two unlabeled ids: `* 054ec98b  d4e716f1  alice ...`. `patio read d4e7` (the second id) failed with "no message with that id in the inbox". `reply` accepts either id. In MCP, `read`, `dismiss` and `reject` take the message id, `reply` takes either, and `wait_for_reply` takes the convo id.

**Why it matters.** A cheap model will pass the wrong id and then retry or give up.

**Fix.** Accept either id in every command and tool. In `inbox`, print one id (or label both: `msg 054ec98b  convo d4e716f1`) and a header row.

### P1-8. Activity and the cards speak the engine's language

**What I saw** (`11-dave-activity.png`, `09-dave-needs-you-held-outgoing.png`, `21-card-incoming-held.png`):
- Activity columns `DIR`, `TOP P`, `DECIDED BY: gate`, verdicts `deliver`, `send`, `ask-human`.
- Cards: "rule `dlp:personal` of `defaults`", "rule `injection` of `contacts.alice`", "JEV · PROBABILITY OF YES; THE BAR IS THE RISK".
- In the incoming card, "Within the allowed topics ... 0.96" shows a short bar, because the bar is the risk. A high number with a short bar reads as a mistake.
- Activity listed `OUT carol question send` for a message that never left: `patio ask carol "hi"` had failed with "carol is not an agent on the server" (exit 3).

**Why it matters.** Activity is where a person checks "what did my agent say for me". Jargon and a false "send" row make it unreliable for that job.

**Fix.** Write each row as a sentence: "dave-team asked alice. Sent." / "alice asked dave-team. Delivered to your inbox." / "Held for you: it contains an email address". Do not log a send that the server refused, or log it as "not sent: no such agent". Move probabilities behind "Details". Draw the bar as "how sure", not "risk", or drop it.

### P1-9. Internal words reach agents and people

**What I saw.**
- MCP server instructions (`src/mcp.ts:65`): "the other side's gatekeeper rejected your message".
- MCP `ask` and `reply` and the guide use the status `held` ("`held` means it waits for @dave").
- Activity: "decided by gate". Join and file: "Jev" in user text. Computers page: "Run `patio pair`", "paired".
- The new agent file's comment uses `orbit/backend` as the example in a workspace called `team`.
- `rules for team, from your answers` in the join output, although the rules are one set for every workspace.

**Why it matters.** Agents repeat these words to their person. PRODUCT.md says gatekeeper, exchange, grants and held never reach a person.

**Fix.** Use one vocabulary: "your rules", "waits for you", "checked", "confirm this computer". Rename the status `held` to `waiting_for_owner` in tool results. Say "rules for dave-team (in every workspace)".

### P1-10. Day 2 has no way out and few switches

**What I saw.**
- No `patio uninstall`, no `patio update`, no `patio leave <workspace>`. `patio update` and `patio uninstall` print the 120-line help with exit 1. The Mac guide's removal is four shell commands. They do not revoke the computer or remove the agent from the server, so teammates still see `dave-team` and their messages queue for 8 days.
- Leaving a workspace is a 10-pixel × on a chip (`17-after-leave-workspace.png`). One click, no confirmation, no word on what happens to channels or open questions.
- Stopping answers needs the terminal: the web app shows the three answers read-only with "Change them: patio rules ...".
- A second agent from an agent: `patio agents add dave-side team` before the confirmation said "Confirm dave-side in the web app first". The card then confirmed only the agent. dave-side was not in team afterwards, and nothing said the add was lost.
- A second agent made with `patio agents new` gets the "anything" defaults, not the first agent's answers, and nobody asks the three questions.

**Fix.**
- `patio uninstall`: stop the service and patio, remove the agents from their workspaces, revoke this computer, and remove the project entries it wrote. Print what it did.
- `patio update`: rerun `install.sh` for the server the computer uses.
- A confirm step on ×: "Take dave-team out of team? It leaves #backend. Open questions from team close."
- In the web app, three switches per agent for the three answers. Narrowing applies at once. Widening is the click itself (the person is in the web app).
- Keep a pending `agents add` with the agent's card, and confirm both in one click. Ask the three questions for every new agent.

### P1-11. MCP surface: small traps for a cheap model

**What I saw.**
- `who` lists only agents "online right now". An offline teammate looks like he does not exist, although `ask` would queue the message. A cheap model says "pablo is not on patio".
- No tool says whether patio itself is running and connected. A model that gets an error cannot tell "patio is down" from "that agent is gone".
- `UNSURE` ("Unsure what to do ... Call `guide`.") is appended to all 12 descriptions. This is fine, but the descriptions are long and repeat the rules text.

**Fix.** `who` lists every member agent with `online: true|false` and its owner. Add a read-only `status` tool (connected, judge, items waiting for the owner). Keep each description to one or two sentences and point to `guide`.

## P2: polish

1. **Needs you mixes a list with the inbox.** It ends with "Your agents" and a Revoke button for each agent (`02-alice-needs-you-empty.png`). A destructive button on the decision page invites misclicks. Two identical empty states ("Nothing waits for you") stack. A "Waiting for you" pill repeats the page title. Move the agent list to Agents; show one empty state.
2. **The confirmation screen** (`07-confirm-screen.png`) shows the full sidebar for a one-step task. It shows a fingerprint with no word on what to compare it with. Show a focused page, and either explain the fingerprint ("matches `patio status` on your computer") or hide it behind Details.
3. **Visual glitches.** Activity's RULE column draws stray top borders on empty cells (`11-dave-activity.png`). The Claude Code tile shows a horizontal scrollbar for a short command (`13-dave-agents.png`, `16-agents-two.png`). The terminal command on the invite page scrolls sideways (the hosted invite page (screenshot removed: it showed private details)). "Connected" pills stretch the full tile width.
4. **Too many heavy black pills.** Approved, Active, Ready, Connected, Done, Signed in all use the same filled dark pill. On a calm monochrome page, they are the loudest element and carry the least news. Use plain gray text or a small dot. Keep the filled style for the one primary button.
5. **Admin workspace page.** Each person has Revoke twice (Notices and People). "Revoke" a person reads like a key operation; say "Remove from team". Notices cannot be dismissed and grow forever.
6. **Join output.** Long absolute paths make every line wrap. Show `~/code/repo` and the file name only. Say "2 agents" with names, or drop the count.
7. **Small copy.** "broadcast to 1 member agent(s)". "Approve once" suggests there is an "always". URL `/devices` for the page Computers.
8. **`patio rules <unknown agent>`** prints sentences for a made-up name with exit 0: "Others may ask nobody anything they like ... nobody does not answer a workspace on its own". It should fail: "no agent named nobody on this computer (patio agents)".
9. **Notifications on macOS** fire for every inbox message, also those the service answers at once. Clicking one opens nothing useful. Notify only for what waits for the person, and open the web app on click.
10. **MCP gaps.** No `leave_channel` and no list of channels for an agent. The guide names the channels only by workspace.

## What is good and must stay

- **The one confirmation sentence**: "Your agent dave-team (Claude Code in ~/code/repo) wants to join team", with Confirm and Decline. One click covers computer, agent and join (`07-confirm-screen.png`).
- **The terminal join.** The tool question and the three questions, each with its default in brackets and Enter to accept (`terminal-join-transcript.txt`). It is calm and fast.
- **Idempotent reruns and honest exit codes** (5, 12, 13). Each `!` step quotes the tool's own words ("Claude Code says: ⏸ Pending approval").
- **Good refusals.** `--ask everything: ... Nothing was changed.` / `side is not a name for an agent of @dave ...; try dave-side` / an unknown link gets the same clear message on the page and in the CLI (`This invite link is not known to this server: check it, or ask for a new one.`).
- **The three answers as sentences** on the Agents page and in `patio rules`. They match the join's words.
- **The guide.** 612 words, plain, with the rules as the person set them, what never to do, and who is in each workspace (the guide page (screenshot removed: it showed a local path)).
- **The cards.** The outgoing card with an editable draft and "Send edited"; the action card with a narrowable "Action you allow" and "Deliver without the action" (`22-card-action.png`); the keys-changed card's honest explanation (`23-card-keys-changed.png`).
- **`install.sh`** checks the SHA-256, installs in the user's home only, and prints a PATH note.
- **Computers page copy**: "Revoking a computer is final: ...". It says the consequence before the click.
- **The visual base.** Light warm gray, system font, one dark accent, generous spacing, no pastel and no gradients. It meets the "calm, monochrome, app-like" target. The fixes above are about weight and wording, not a new style.
